Filling the unseen

SHARP can only show what the photo saw. Move the camera and everything behind the truck or past the frame edge is black or smeared. Here are seven ways to handle those areas along the same camera sweep. Pick a method, drag across the image to move the camera, or put two methods side by side. Filled content only has to look believable and hold still as you move.

What to look for

Drag slowly toward either end. Per-frame SDXL looks cleanest in any single frame, but its fill changes every frame. The 3D fills barely shimmer, but show grey ghosting right behind foreground objects, like the truck's cab.

Why the 3D fills ghost

They're painted from new camera positions, where the area behind the truck is already SHARP's grey edge smear, so SDXL continues the grey. The four-frame version adds visible jumps when it switches fill frames, which is why it scores worse than the two-frame version.

What the trained layer shows

Predicting the hidden layer directly gives the steadiest fill here (7.3): being 3D, it cannot change as you drag. Its content still loses to SHARP's own smear on held-out scenes (error 0.163 vs 0.144; perceptual 0.414 vs 0.362). Training on 3.5× more data changed that by 0.001, so the limit is the from-scratch appearance model, not the data.

What is actually hidden

Every pixel the source photo never saw, classified by what was in the way: content past the photo's edges, background a foreground object was covering, or that object's own surface curving out of view. This decides where effort belongs — regenerating the far side of objects addresses almost nothing at this kind of sideways motion, while extending past the frame and completing background behind objects covers nearly all of it.
